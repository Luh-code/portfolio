Project Jul 2026 · Final-year project

Mukuba University Helpdesk & Service Management System

Team of four · my part: mostly front end · PHP 8, MySQL

One web platform for ICT support tickets and the university's paper-based, multi-office approval processes.

What I built
In a team of four, I worked mostly on the front end: the responsive interface that students and staff use. Together we built a system that combines ICT support ticketing (categories, SLA priorities, live chat, an FAQ) with one shared approval-workflow engine. That engine runs six administrative processes: graduation clearance, deregistration, withdrawal, programme change, academic appeals and transcript requests.
Why it exists
It was our final-year project. We designed it after interviewing ICT, department and finance staff and watching how the paper process actually worked.
The problem
Requests travelled as paper forms carried between up to six offices: Head of Department, Dean, ICT, Dean of Students, Bursar and Finance. Forms got lost, students couldn't tell which office had their file, management had no record of how long each office took, and nobody outside the university could check whether a clearance certificate or receipt was genuine.
The value
Students see in real time which office holds their request. Each office sees only what's waiting on it, and every decision is logged with who made it and when. Approval rights follow the office rather than the person, so a new Head of Department can approve on day one with no reconfiguration. Appeals and transcripts wait for MTN or Airtel mobile-money payment (tested against the providers' sandboxes), and anyone, an employer for example, can scan a document's QR code to confirm it's real. Every unit, integration, security and acceptance test passed; a live pilot at the university is the next step.

Project Jul 2026 · Side project

Memory Frame Designer

Solo, built with AI coding assistants · Code on GitHub for Memory Frame Designer

What I built
A browser-based editor for printable photo keepsakes. You pick a template (an anniversary or a Valentine's design), drop photos into a heart collage, edit the names, highlight a date on a calendar, add a Spotify music code, and export a print-quality PNG or PDF.
Why it exists
A side project, built for fun. AI assistants wrote most of the code; I decided what it should do and fixed it up until it worked on desktop and mobile.
The problem
Personalised frames like these are usually sold as templates you have to edit in design software, which most people don't have or know how to use.
The value
Anyone can make a print-ready gift in a few minutes by filling in the blanks. No design skills or software needed.
